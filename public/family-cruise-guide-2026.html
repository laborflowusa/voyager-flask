The Lines
Which Cruise Line Is Right for Your Family?

Not all cruise lines are built the same. Here's the honest breakdown by age group and travel style.

Royal Caribbean
Best for: Tweens, Teens & Thrill Seekers
The most activity-packed ships afloat. Surf simulators, rock climbing walls, go-karts, waterslides, and the largest kids clubs in the industry. If your kids need stimulation, this is your line.
  • Perfect Day at CocoCay — private island, massive waterpark
  • Adventure Ocean kids club — infants through teens
  • Icon of the Seas — most amenities of any ship ever built
Disney Cruise Line
Best for: Young Kids (Under 10)
Premium priced but worth it for families with young children. Character experiences, age-specific clubs from 3 months up, and adult-only areas for parents. The gold standard for under-10 cruising.
  • Character meet-and-greets every day of the cruise
  • Oceaneers Club — the best kids club at sea, period
  • Castaway Cay — Disney's private island in the Bahamas
Norwegian Cruise Line
Best for: Flexible Families, Multi-Gen Groups
Freestyle dining means no set dinner times — perfect for families with unpredictable kids. Strong onboard entertainment, good value, and the "Free at Sea" promotions can save families hundreds.
  • Freestyle dining — eat when and where you want
  • Free at Sea — specialty dining, drink packages, excursions
  • Haven suites for multi-generational groups needing space
Carnival Cruise Line
Best for: Budget Families, First Cruisers
The most affordable major line with genuinely good family programming. Camp Ocean kids clubs, waterslides on every ship, and Caribbean itineraries from nearly every major East and Gulf Coast port.
  • Most affordable entry point for family cruising
  • Camp Ocean — structured programming for all ages
  • Departs from more US ports than any other line
MSC Cruises
Best for: Mediterranean, International Experience
Europe's largest cruise line and the best choice for Mediterranean itineraries. Kids sail free on many sailings, and the European service style gives families a genuinely different experience.
  • Kids sail free — frequently runs on European itineraries
  • Best Mediterranean port selection of any line
  • MSC Yacht Club for multigenerational groups wanting luxury
Holland America
Best for: Multi-Gen, Alaska, Older Kids
The best Alaska cruise line, full stop. More time in port, slower pace, and a mature onboard culture that works perfectly for multi-generational trips where grandparents are in the group.
  • Finest Alaska itineraries — Glacier Bay, Denali extensions
  • Club HAL kids program well-suited to 8–17
  • Quieter, more relaxed atmosphere for mixed-age groups
By Age Group
What Works for Every Member of Your Family

The biggest mistake families make is booking without thinking about what each age group actually needs on a ship.

👶
Little Ones
Ages 0–9
  • Disney Cruise Line is the only right answer for under 5
  • Royal Caribbean Adventure Ocean for ages 3–11
  • Carnival Camp Ocean from age 2
  • Shallow splash zones and kiddie pools on all major ships
  • Nap schedules still work — ship routine is predictable
Pro tip: Book a stateroom with a bathtub, not just a shower. With young kids it makes evenings infinitely easier. Not all cabins have them — call to confirm.
🎮
Tweens & Teens
Ages 10–17
  • Royal Caribbean — go-karts, surf simulators, escape rooms
  • Norwegian — teen-specific clubs with more independence
  • Shore excursions become genuinely exciting at this age
  • Zip-lining in Juneau, ATV in Cozumel, snorkeling in Nassau
  • Teen clubs open until midnight — parents get evenings back
Pro tip: Give teens a small daily spending budget on their cruise card. It teaches money skills and removes constant negotiation. Most lines allow this setup at check-in.
👴
Multi-Generational
Grandparents + Everyone
  • Norwegian Haven suites — private complex, butler service
  • Holland America — quieter, accessible, slower paced
  • Connecting staterooms essential — book early, they sell out
  • Mobility considerations: all lines ADA accessible
  • Shared dinner as a group every night — the magic of cruising
Pro tip: On multi-gen trips, book a private shore excursion rather than the ship's group tours. A private van for your family moves at your pace and nobody is left behind.
Before You Board
Nine Things Every Family Should Know
📅
Book 6 Months Out Minimum
Connecting cabins, specific deck locations, and early dining times disappear fast. For summer and holiday sailings, 9–12 months is the right timeline. The itinerary stays the same — the cabin quality does not.
🍕
The Main Dining Room Is Free
Every ship has a main dining room included in your fare with a full menu, tableside service, and the same food quality as specialty restaurants. Skip the specialty dining upsell until your second cruise — the included food is genuinely excellent.
📱
Buy the Drink Package Before Boarding
Drink packages (including non-alcoholic for kids) are always 10–20% cheaper if purchased before the sailing date through the cruise line app. Onboard pricing is full retail. Book it the moment it opens — usually 90 days out.
🌊
Sea Day Strategy
On sea days, the main pool deck is chaos by 10am. The adults-only areas are almost empty and right next to the family pool. For multi-gen trips, send grandparents there while parents rotate supervising the kids. Everyone wins.
🏝️
Book Shore Excursions Early
The whale watching in Juneau, the private beach in Nassau, the ruins tour in Cozumel — they have capacity limits. Book the moment bookings open, usually 90 days before sailing. The best excursions sell out weeks ahead of departure.
💊
Sea Sickness Is Real — Prepare
Even on large ships in calm Caribbean waters, some kids (and adults) get motion sick. Pack Dramamine or Bonine and the behind-ear motion sickness patches. Midship cabins on lower decks feel the least movement. Don't find this out on day one.
💰
The "Kids Sail Free" Math
Kids sail free deals are real but read the fine print. The third and fourth passenger rate (which is what kids cruise at) is often already heavily discounted. Compare the "kids free" promotional rate against the standard family rate — sometimes the deal is genuine, sometimes it isn't.
🎒
Pack a Day Bag for Each Port
Your big luggage stays in the cabin. For every port day, each family member needs a small bag: sunscreen, water, ID, spending money, medication, and a change of clothes. You cannot return to the ship for forgotten items — the ship won't wait.
🛳️
Arrive the Day Before Embarkation
Flying in the morning of your cruise is the single biggest risk in family cruise planning. One delayed flight = missed ship. The ship does not wait. Always stay overnight in the embarkation city. This is non-negotiable advice.

Know Before You Book
What's Included — and What Isn't

The word "all-inclusive" on a cruise means something specific — and something limited. Know the difference before you board.

✅ Always Included

  • Main dining room — breakfast, lunch, dinner
  • Buffet restaurant — all day, every day
  • Kids club enrollment and programming
  • Onboard entertainment (shows, movies, activities)
  • Swimming pools and waterslides
  • Fitness center access
  • Port taxes and fees (usually)

💳 Usually Costs Extra

  • Specialty restaurants ($20–65/person)
  • Alcoholic beverages and sodas
  • Shore excursions
  • Spa treatments
  • Wi-Fi (expect $20–30/day per device)
  • Photos from the ship photographer
  • Gratuities ($18–22/person/day)
Budget reality check: For a family of four on a 7-night cruise, add $400–600 for gratuities, $200–400 for Wi-Fi and beverages, and $300–600 for shore excursions. Budget these in before you book — the sticker price on the cabin is not your final cost.

Questions Answered
Straight Talk on Family Cruising
What age is too young to cruise?
Most cruise lines accept infants as young as 6 months old. Disney Cruise Line accepts babies from 3 months. The real question isn't age — it's temperament. If your baby does well with routine and new environments, a cruise works well. Toddlers ages 2–4 are arguably the sweetest age to cruise — the ship is endlessly stimulating for them and they sleep hard. The bigger challenge is connecting cabins, which book out fast.
Do kids need a passport to cruise?
For closed-loop Caribbean cruises — meaning the ship departs and returns to the same US port — US citizens can travel with an original birth certificate and government-issued ID instead of a passport. However, a passport is strongly recommended because if a medical emergency requires you to fly home from a foreign port, you need one. For any cruise that crosses into international waters and returns to a different port, a passport is required.
Is a cruise actually cheaper than a resort vacation?
Almost always yes, when you compare apples to apples. A 7-night Caribbean cruise for a family of four at $3,000 includes every meal, entertainment, accommodation, and travel between multiple destinations. The equivalent week at a decent all-inclusive resort in Cancun runs $4,500–6,000 for less variety and one location. The cruise wins on value, especially once kids are old enough for the clubs.
What if my kids get seasick?
Modern cruise ships are massive stabilized vessels — a Caribbean sailing on a modern ship in average weather barely registers motion. That said, some children (and adults) are sensitive. Book a midship cabin on a lower deck — this is where the ship moves least. Pack behind-ear scopolamine patches and over-the-counter Bonine. If your child is severely prone to motion sickness, consider the newest, largest ships which have the most stabilization technology.
How do I find the best family cruise deals?
Three strategies work consistently. Book early — 6 to 9 months out — for the best cabin selection at base rates. Book during wave season (January through March) when cruise lines run their deepest annual promotions. And use CruiseDirect to compare across all lines simultaneously rather than checking each line individually. The third and fourth passenger rate is often the variable — compare that number carefully across lines for the same itinerary.
What's the best Caribbean cruise port to depart from?
For most East Coast families, Port Canaveral (Orlando) and Miami are the top choices — large selection of ships, competitive pricing, and easy to combine with a theme park visit before or after. For Southeast families, Port Canaveral plus a Disney World day is unbeatable value. For Texas and Midwest families, Galveston's Gulf Coast access makes Carnival sailings especially affordable. The port closest to you almost always wins on total trip cost once you factor in driving vs. flying.
